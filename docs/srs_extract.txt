1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the functional and non-functional requirements, system 
models, architecture, and delivery plan for OneHealth — a mobile and web platform that connects patients with private-
practice doctors (consultants) and their dispensaries in Sri Lanka. The document is written to be directly usable by a two-
person development team to design, build, and deploy a production system; it is not an academic or university-project 
specification.
1.2 Scope
OneHealth allows a doctor to register a private dispensary (channelling centre) on the platform after identity and 
credential verification, publish its location and operating hours, and let patients discover the dispensary, view 
ratings/reviews, and reserve a queue (channelling) number remotely. Patients can track their queue number in real time 
instead of waiting physically at the dispensary or guessing when to return. The platform is offered through three 
subscription packages for doctors — Free, Standard, and Premium — each unlocking a progressively richer set of 
features (staff accounts, online payment and invoicing, e-prescriptions, and full electronic medical records). The system 
also includes an administrative back office for verifying doctors, moderating reviews, and managing billing, and a 
lightweight AI recommendation feature that suggests suitable doctors based on patient-entered symptoms and 
accumulated review data.
1.3 Problem Statement
The requirement originates from a real, recurring problem in Sri Lanka's private outpatient (channelling) system: a 
patient who is unwell — for example, with a fever — but away from their usual area does not know which nearby 
private medical centres are reputable. Even after locating a dispensary, the day's queue numbers may already be 
exhausted by the time the patient arrives, so no consultation or medicine can be obtained that day. Separately, once a 
number is issued, some patients leave and return later expecting to keep their place, and by the time they come back their 
number may already have been called and passed. OneHealth directly targets this problem by letting patients discover 
verified nearby doctors, see live queue status, and reserve a number before travelling, while giving doctors and their staff 
a simple tool to manage the queue, payments, and patient records digitally instead of relying on paper token boards or 
informal channels such as WhatsApp.
1.4 Definitions, Acronyms and Abbreviations
Term Definition
Dispensary A doctor's private consultation/channelling centre where patients are seen and, in most 
cases, medicine is dispensed on site.
Queue Number / Token The sequential number assigned to a patient for a specific doctor on a specific day, used to 
determine consultation order.
Staff Member A receptionist or pharmacist granted delegated access by a doctor under the Standard or 
Premium package.
SLMC Sri Lanka Medical Council — the statutory body whose registration number is used to 
verify a doctor's licence.

instead of handwritten paper or WhatsApp.

under the Premium package.

2. Overall Description
2.1 Product Perspective
OneHealth is a new, standalone platform consisting of a cross-platform mobile app (patients and doctors), a responsive 
web portal (doctors and administrators), a backend API, and a small AI micro-service for doctor recommendation. It is 
not a module of an existing hospital or government system; it is built to onboard private-practice doctors independently 
and can later integrate with national e-health initiatives if required.
2.2 Product Features Summary
• Doctor onboarding with certificate/licence verification by an admin before the profile goes live.
• Dispensary registration with map location, city/town, and working hours.
• Patient search and discovery by doctor name, city, or town, with ratings and reviews.
• Remote queue (channelling) number booking with a live, real-time queue counter.
• Push/SMS notification as a patient's number approaches.
• Tiered packages (Free / Standard / Premium) unlocking staff accounts, online payment & invoicing, e-
prescriptions, and full electronic medical records.
• Post-visit feedback that also trains an AI recommender to suggest suitable doctors from symptoms.
• Admin console for verification, review moderation, and subscription/billing management.

2.4 Operating Environment
• Patient/Doctor mobile app: Android 8+ and iOS 14+ via a single React Native codebase.
• Doctor/Admin web portal: modern evergreen browsers (Chrome, Edge, Safari, Firefox), responsive down to tablet 
width.
• Backend: containerised Node.js service deployable to any cloud VM or PaaS (Render, Railway, AWS).
• Network: designed for Sri Lankan mobile-data conditions — payloads kept small, images compressed, and offline-
tolerant queue-status caching on the client.
2.5 Design and Implementation Constraints
• Must be deliverable as a rapid MVP by two developers — favour managed services and proven libraries over 
custom infrastructure.
• Doctor identity/credential verification is mandatory before any dispensary becomes publicly bookable (patient 
safety and trust requirement).

3. Subscription Packages
Doctors choose one of three packages when registering a dispensary on OneHealth. Every package includes the core 
discovery, booking, and live-queue experience for patients; higher packages add staff delegation, payments, and clinical 
record-keeping.
3.1 Free Plan
Designed for a solo doctor testing the platform. The doctor is the only account holder — no staff/administrator accounts 
can be added. The doctor personally updates the current queue number from the mobile app or web portal. Patients can 
search, view the doctor's profile, ratings and reviews, and book a queue number; only in-person (cash/card at counter) 
payment is supported — no online payment integration at this tier.
3.2 Standard Plan (Paid)
Everything in Free, plus the ability to add staff accounts (receptionist and/or pharmacist) with scoped permissions. Staff 
can be delegated the right to update the live queue number on the doctor's behalf. This tier activates online payment for 
queue bookings, automatic e-invoice generation, and the ability for the doctor to send an e-prescription directly to the 
pharmacist role (removing the informal WhatsApp workaround many dispensaries currently use).
Additional Standard-tier features recommended for the real-world rollout:
• Daily/weekly booking and revenue summary for the doctor.
• Configurable daily queue capacity (maximum tokens per day) per dispensary.
• Multi-staff shift assignment (e.g., morning/evening receptionist).
3.3 Premium Plan (Paid)
Everything in Standard, plus full electronic medical record (EMR) management: the doctor can enter and view a patient's 
diagnosis history, and both doctor and patient can securely upload and exchange reports and documents inside the app 
instead of informal channels. The doctor can review a patient's past visit history at the point of consultation.
Additional Premium-tier features recommended for the real-world rollout:
• Multi-dispensary / multi-branch management for doctors who practise at more than one centre.
• Priority placement in AI doctor-recommendation results (clearly labelled as sponsored/priority, not hidden).
• Exportable patient visit summary (PDF) for referrals to other specialists.

6. Non-Functional Requirements
Category Requirement
Performance
Queue-status updates must propagate to a patient's device within 3 seconds of a staff/doctor 
update, via the real-time (Socket.IO) channel. Search results shall return within 2 seconds 
under normal 3G/4G conditions.
Scalability The backend shall be stateless and horizontally scalable behind the API gateway; Redis 
shall back the real-time layer so multiple backend instances can share live queue state.
Availability Target 99.5% uptime for the booking and live-queue services during business hours (7am–
9pm local time), the busiest period for private dispensaries.
Security
Passwords stored with bcrypt/argon2 hashing; all traffic over HTTPS/TLS; JWT access 
tokens with short expiry plus refresh tokens; role-based access control enforced on every 
API route.
Data Privacy
Medical records and reports (Premium) are accessible only to the owning patient and their 
treating doctor; all access is logged. This aligns with the sensitivity expected for personal 
health information even ahead of any formal certification.
Usability
The patient booking flow (search → view profile → book number) must be completable in 
under 5 taps/screens on the mobile app, since the target user may be unwell and have low 
patience for a complex flow.
Localization UI text externalised for English, Sinhala, and Tamil from the first release, since 
dispensaries and patients across Sri Lanka use all three languages.
Reliability Queue-number updates and payment transactions must be idempotent and resistant to 
double-submission (e.g., a patient tapping "Book" twice must not create two tokens).
Maintainability
Codebase organised into clear modules per domain (auth, queue, payments, records) as 
detailed in the accompanying File & Development Structure annex, enabling two 
developers to work in parallel with minimal merge conflicts.
Compatibility Mobile app supports Android 8+ and iOS 14+; web portal supports the last two major 
versions of Chrome, Edge, Safari, and Firefox.
Auditability All admin actions (approve/reject doctor, suspend account, moderate review) are recorded 
with timestamp and admin identity for dispute resolution.

7. System Architecture
The architecture favours managed services and well-documented, mainstream libraries so that a two-developer team can 
ship a production MVP quickly without operating heavy custom infrastructure.
7.1 Architecture Overview
Figure 7.1 — OneHealth System Architecture
Both the mobile app and web portal talk to a single NGINX gateway in front of a Node.js application layer split into 
domain services (auth, queue, doctor/dispensary, payments, records). A Socket.IO realtime gateway, backed by Redis 
pub/sub, pushes live queue-number updates to connected devices. PostgreSQL is the system of record; an object store 
(S3-compatible or Firebase Storage) holds certificates, uploaded reports, and prescriptions. A small Python/FastAPI 
micro-service handles the AI doctor-recommendation feature independently of the core booking flow so it can be 
iterated on without touching booking-critical code.

7.3 Database Design & Connection
PostgreSQL is accessed exclusively through Prisma ORM from the Node.js backend, using a pooled connection 
(PgBouncer or Prisma's own connection pool) to handle bursts of queue-booking traffic without exhausting database 
connections. Configuration (host, port, credentials, pool size) is supplied via environment variables and never committed 
to source control; separate connection strings are used for local, staging, and production, matching the environments 
defined in the accompanying File & Development Structure annex.
• Base table: users (shared columns for all actor types) with a discriminator user_type column, extended by one-to-
one doctors, patients, staff, admins tables, mirroring the EER generalisation in Figure 4.6.
• dispensaries table references doctors and stores city/town plus latitude/longitude for map search and "nearby 
doctor" queries (PostGIS extension optional if radius search is needed later).
• queue_tokens table is the hottest table at runtime; it is indexed on (dispensary_id, queue_date, token_number) to 
make "current number" lookups and "next available number" calculations fast.
• payments and invoices are linked 1:1 to a queue_token so every paid booking has a traceable financial record.
• medical_records and prescriptions carry a visibility/ownership scope enforced at the application layer (Premium 
feature) so only the owning patient and treating doctor can read them.
• reviews are linked to both doctor and patient, with a moderation_status column the admin can flip to hide abusive 
content without deleting the underlying record (audit-friendly).
Full table structures and cardinalities are shown in the ER diagram (Figure 4.5) and the specialisation hierarchy in the 
EER diagram (Figure 4.6).

8. Delivery Plan for a Two-Developer Team
OneHealth is scoped as a rapid MVP, not a long-running project. The two developers are expected to work from a single 
monorepo with a clear role split, described fully in the companion document “OneHealth – File & Development 
Structure” (supplied as both a compiled PDF and an editable LaTeX .tex source). This section summarises the key 
decisions relevant to requirement delivery.
8.1 Role Split
Developer A — Backend & Data Developer B — Frontend (Mobile + Web)
Node.js/Express API, PostgreSQL schema & Prisma 
migrations, authentication, queue logic, payment gateway 
integration, Socket.IO realtime layer, AI micro-service 
wiring, CI/CD and deployment.
React Native (Expo) patient/doctor app, React (Vite) web 
portal for doctor/admin, shared UI kit, API integration, 
push notification handling, and the booking/live-queue 
UX.
Both developers jointly own the shared TypeScript types/validation package, since it is the contract between frontend 
and backend.
8.2 Suggested Rapid Delivery Timeline
Phase Focus
Week 1–2 Auth, base user/doctor/patient models, doctor registration with certificate upload, 
dispensary CRUD.
Week 3–4 Free-plan flow end-to-end: search, profile view, book token, live queue number via 
Socket.IO, review & rating.
Week 5–6 Standard plan: staff accounts & permissions, online payment, invoicing, e-prescription 
hand-off to pharmacist.
Week 7–8 Premium plan: patient medical records, doctor report upload/view, secure report sharing.
Week 9 AI recommendation service (symptom-to-doctor suggestion) using accumulated 
review/feedback data.
Week 10 Admin console (verification, moderation, billing), hardening, staging QA, production 
deployment.
This timeline targets a launch-ready Free + Standard experience within roughly six weeks, with Premium and AI 
features following as fast-follow releases rather than blocking the initial go-live — appropriate for validating the product 
with a small number of pilot dispensaries before wider rollout.

• Free / Standard / Premium — the three doctor subscription packages defined in Section 3.
• Real-time queue number — the currently-being-served token number, pushed live to waiting patients.
• AI Recommender — a lightweight service that suggests doctors from patient-entered symptoms and historical 
review data; explicitly not a diagnostic tool.
9.2 Out of Scope for MVP
• Integration with national e-health/insurance systems.
• Video/tele-consultation (may be considered as a future package add-on).
• In-app pharmacy inventory management beyond marking a prescription as dispensed.
